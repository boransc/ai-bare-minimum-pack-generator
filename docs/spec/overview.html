The Bare Minimum Pack Generator

A short web tool that tells an organisation whether its use of AI is under minimum control, and hands it the four documents that fix the gap.

Three-day buildFive screensNo login1 September 2026

What it is

One honest answer, and the paperwork to act on it

An organisation answers a few questions about itself, then works through Governance AI's eight-point AI Minimum Standard. It immediately gets a straight verdict on whether the minimum is in place, the exact things it cannot yet evidence, and a version of the Bare Minimum Pack written with its own situation in mind.

It is for the person who runs a small or mid-sized organisation, knows their staff are already using AI, and has no idea whether anyone is governing it. Ten minutes, no account, nothing to buy.

The screens

Five screens, in the order a person meets them

01

The front page

What this is, what they will get, and how long it takes.

One button: start. A short note that no account is needed and they can come back to their pack later using a private link.

02

Eight questions about them

One question per step, all multiple choice.

Their sector, how many people work there, how much AI is being used and what for, whether it touches confidential or personal information, whether they are regulated, whether AI plays any part in decisions about people, and whether anyone senior is actually accountable for it. Organisation name is optional.

Nothing sensitive is ever asked. "Don't know" is a real answer on most of them, and it is treated as something to find out rather than a wrong answer.

03

The eight-point check

The assessment itself. Eight cards, one per point.

Each card holds the two or three specific statements the Standard actually asks about, and each is answered yes or no. A point only counts as a yes when every statement under it is a yes, and a yes means there is real evidence behind it, not a good intention.

04

Their result and their pack

The whole point of the product, on one long page.

It opens with the verdict in plain words: the minimum is in place, or it is not. The score out of eight sits underneath it, not above it. If either red-line point has failed, that is called out before anything else.

Below that: what to do first, the full point-by-point breakdown showing exactly which statements are missing, the thirty-day plan, the policy template and the staff note. Then a print button, a private link to come back to — copy it, or have it emailed — and a route into the full governance review.

The email is optional, carries the link and nothing else, and unlocks nothing. Marketing consent is a separate box, unticked.

05

The same pack, later

What their private link opens.

Exactly the page they saw before, unchanged, with its original date on it. The link works for ninety days and the saved pack is deleted after a year. Anyone holding the link can open it, and they are told so.

One journey

A housing association, start to finish

  1. A director follows a link from a Governance AI newsletter and lands on the front page. She reads two paragraphs and presses start.
  2. She answers the eight questions in about ninety seconds. Housing. Sixty staff. AI used regularly in some teams. Drafting, data analysis, meeting notes. It touches tenant information routinely. They are regulated. It plays a part in decisions about people. Nobody senior owns it.
  3. She works through the eight points. Some she can answer straight away. On the data rules she pauses, realises nothing has ever been written down, and answers no honestly.
  4. She presses Generate my pack and waits a few seconds.
  5. The page opens with The minimum is not met. Underneath: five of eight points evidenced. Directly below that, a marked block explaining that the data rules and the human check are red lines, and a no against either means the minimum is not met whatever the total says.
  6. The opening paragraph describes her own situation back to her, and a short scenario underneath it shows the shape of how this goes wrong for an organisation like hers.
  7. She scrolls through the four documents, prints the pack for Thursday's board meeting, and copies her private link into her calendar for next month.
  8. At the bottom she sees why the fuller review is being recommended to her specifically: no senior owner, and AI touching decisions about people. She books a call.

The design decision that matters most: a seven-out-of-eight organisation that fails the human-check point is told plainly that the minimum is not met. The number never overrides the red line. That honesty is the whole reason anyone would trust the result.

Under the hood

What happens when they press Generate my pack

The answers go to the server in one request.Nothing is worked out in the browser, so the scoring rules cannot be poked at from outside.
The score is worked out by ordinary code, not by AI.Each point passes only if every statement under it passes. Then the red-line rule is applied, then the band. The verdict is decided before anything else happens.
The gaps are turned into an ordered list of actions.Red lines first, then Karl's own thirty-day sequence. The order comes from the source document, never from a model.
A small, tightly fenced request goes to the AI.It is shown only the relevant paragraphs of the source document and the multiple-choice answers. It never sees the regulator's name, never sees free text, and is asked for a handful of short, capped sentences: an opening paragraph, a risk scenario, and one line per failed point.
Everything it returns is checked before anyone sees it.Wrong shape, too long, or any sign of an invented rule — a named law, a regulator, a deadline, a "you must" — and that sentence is thrown away and replaced with the source document's own wording. The page always renders. The worst case is a pack that is less personal, never one that is wrong.
The pack is assembled from fixed content plus those few sentences.The eight points, the policy and the staff note are reproduced exactly as written. The AI has no way to alter them, because it is never asked for them.
The finished pack is saved under a long random link and returned in the same response.The page renders from what came back, so it never has to wait for the save to settle. The saved copy deletes itself after a year.

Three days

What exists at the end of each day

Day one

The honest answer, with no AI in it

The source document is turned into structured content in the repo. The eight questions and the eight-point check are built as one stepped form. The scoring, the red-line override and the verdict are written and tested properly.

At the end of the daySomeone can complete the whole thing and get a correct, honest result on screen. It looks plain and there is no tailoring, but every number and every verdict is right.

Day two

The pack, and the tailoring

The four documents render in full. The AI call goes in, with its checks and its fallbacks, and is tested against nonsense and against deliberate attempts to make it invent a rule. The print layout is built.

At the end of the dayA complete, tailored, printable pack. Turn the AI off and it still works — that is the test that matters.

Day three

Saving, coming back, and polish

The private link, emailing it on request, the ninety-day and twelve-month lifetimes, a cap so nobody can hammer the AI, keyboard and screen-reader passes, and the wording that tells people plainly what is stored and who can see it.

Email sends from a test address until Governance AI adds the DNS records for their own domain. That is their dependency, not a blocker.

At the end of the daySomething you can put in front of a real organisation.

Not building

Deliberately left out

Word downloads
Printing gives them a PDF. Getting the policy into their own template can wait.
A second AI pass
Checking the AI's work with more AI doubles the cost and the wait. The plain checks catch what matters. Earn it later.
Saving ticked boxes
The checklist prints and can be worked through on paper. Remembering which boxes are ticked is next week's problem.
An admin dashboard
Everything it would need is being stored. Read it out of storage until there is enough of it to be worth a screen.
Lead indexes and analytics
Machinery for a scale that does not exist yet. Five real users will teach more.
Re-taking the assessment over time
Worth building once people are actually coming back. The stored data is shaped so it can be added without a rewrite.

Still open

Two things only the client can settle

Someone at Governance AI has to confirm that the structured version of the Pack is a faithful copy of the original, before it goes anywhere real. Every generated document inherits it.

And nobody has yet said whether coming back to a saved pack should push the ninety days further out, or whether the link simply dies ninety days after it was made. The build assumes it dies.
